Technical names and notation

The model calls the first family ancestor—descendant, written ad(m), where m is the number of unsampled infections between A and B. It calls the second family common ancestor, written ca(mA,mB), where the two numbers count unsampled infections on the routes to A and B.

ad(0) means direct transmission. ca(0,0) means one unsampled source directly infected both A and B. "Hidden" or "latent" here means unobserved in the data; it does not refer to the biological latent period.

How biology and genome change enter the model

Infection and testing assumptions describe the variable time from infection to symptoms, onward transmission, and sampling. These affect the expected gap between sample dates.

The teaching data use EpiLink's standard parameters, informed by a published COVID-19 infectiousness model.

Molecular-clock assumptions describe how quickly virus genomes change. More time and a faster clock generally allow more differences to accumulate.

The standard genome length is 29,903 positions. These settings are illustrative and are not automatically suitable for every disease or dataset.

How the compatibility score is calculated

For each story, EpiLink asks where the observed sample-date gap and genome difference sit in their respective simulated distributions. An observation near the middle can receive a value near 1 for that clue; observations toward either edge approach 0.

The two clue scores are multiplied to give one compatibility score per story. Scores for selected stories are then added. This is why a combined score may exceed 1—and why combined scores based on different numbers of stories should not be compared directly.

Use with care

What this explorer cannot tell you

  • It cannot identify who infected whom.
  • It does not include contact, location, or exposure evidence.
  • Its result depends on the stories and simplified model assumptions considered.
  • It is a teaching tool, not a clinical or public-health decision system.
