# Suggested Reviewers - PLOS Computational Biology Submission

Verified and ranked on 2026-05-26 after reviewing the main manuscript and appendix.
The ranking prioritises fit to EpiLink's core contributions: process-based recent-transmission
compatibility, genomic clustering, pairwise transmission-event evaluation, molecular-clock
assumptions, graph/community clustering, and public-health genomic surveillance.

PLOS Computational Biology requires at least 4 suggested reviewers. The 10 primary names below
give enough coverage to absorb likely invitations being declined. Before submission, the
corresponding author should still do a final conflict check against recent co-authorship,
supervision, institutional ties, and active collaborations.

## Final Conflict and Suitability Notes

- Initial screening did not identify obvious direct co-authorship between the 10 candidates and Dominic Arthur, Christopher J. Banks, or Rowland R. Kao within the last 3 years. This was a targeted screen, not a definitive bibliometric audit.
- Several candidates are cited in the manuscript because they developed comparison methods or relevant clustering/phylodynamic approaches. Citations alone are not normally a competing interest, but mention this in the submission form if PLOS asks for context.
- Avoid suggesting current University of Edinburgh reviewers unless PLOS specifically permits them and no conflict exists. This is why otherwise relevant Edinburgh-based phylodynamics candidates were not included.
- Candidates linked to the Boston dataset itself, such as Jacob Lemieux, Pardis Sabeti, William Hanage, or Tami Lieberman, were not prioritised because the manuscript reanalyses their published dataset; they would be more natural as subject-matter commentators than independent reviewers of the empirical validation.
- If only 4-6 names are entered, use ranks 1-6. If PLOS wants institutional diversity, use ranks 1-5 plus either 6 or 9, not both Imperial candidates.
